How kōan compares

This is kōan's apps and server lined up against the players and servers people use alongside it or instead of it. I checked every cell in October 2026 against each project's own site, store listing, docs or source code, and kōan's own column against its source, including stuff in open pull requests.

Yes: a source says it does. Partly: with the limit noted. No: a source says it does not, or a complete feature list leaves out something that would be on it. ?: not confirmed either way. Soon: in an open kōan pull request. Planned: on kōan's roadmap, not yet started. Products change faster than this page does, so if a cell's wrong, an issue is very welcome.

Where kōan is different

Correction files work as they are. kōan reads what room and headphone tools actually produce: AutoEQ, Equalizer APO configs with their includes, CamillaDSP YAML with mixers and delays, REW exports, Convolver .cfg files and Roon's zips of impulse responses, one per sample rate. A profile belongs to an output, so your headphones only get their correction when they're the thing playing, and an output without one stays bit-perfect. On the desktop, Roon and Audirvana Studio run convolution, and JRiver and HQPlayer take impulse responses. On a phone, Symfonium, Poweramp, Neutron and NaviBeat take AutoEQ, but none of them runs an impulse response. kōan does both on iOS, and can send a processed stream to a UPnP amp.

Devices are remotes for each other. Any kōan app can control another one, directly on the same network or from anywhere through a kōan server, and move what's playing over to it. Jellyfin, Plex, Emby and Roon control their own sessions; among the Subsonic apps, NaviBeat carries playback on to another device, Shelv and Arpeggi sync the queue, and Tempus has started pairing devices on one network.

Signing in without typing a password. An invite link signs a device in with a key of its own; a device without a keyboard shows a code that a signed-in phone, Mac or browser approves; and apps that only speak Subsonic's password token get their own app password each. Jellyfin's Quick Connect and plex.tv/link are the nearest things. Subsonic servers that accept token sign-in usually keep your password somewhere it can be read back; kōan keeps a hash and hands out app passwords instead.

An assistant can drive it. A kōan server is an MCP server with its own sign-in. None of the other servers here ship one.

What it doesn't do (yet). No Android app, CarPlay or Apple Watch app. No crossfade. No Chromecast or Sonos. One server and one library at a time. No Jellyfin or Plex. No radio, mixes, podcasts or internet radio. No Last.fm scrobbling. No widgets or Siri. Tag editing's planned, and the Apple TV app is in an open pull request.
