kōan

Your player, your server, your remote and your EQ, on every device you listen on.

One Rust engine runs on macOS, Linux, iPhone, Apple TV, Android, a terminal and a server. It plays folders on your computer, a kōan server, or any OpenSubsonic server such as Navidrome.

Your player

The desktop app runs on macOS and Linux, and the same app runs on iPhone. It is drawn with GPUI, the GPU framework built for the Zed editor, and links the audio engine directly.

The parts that move are shaders: the colour wash from the record's sleeve, the playing bars, the seek bar, the lyrics and the answer to a press. They run at the display's refresh rate, full screen on a 4K display holds 120 fps, and nothing is drawn while nothing moves.

  • Bit-perfect and gapless, where the system allows. The output switches to each track's sample rate.
  • Folders and a server in one library. On the desktop, a track you have on disk plays from the file.
  • No signal needed. A phone fetches the queue ahead and keeps what it plays.

New in the GPUI apps

  • The iPhone app is now the GPUI app. It updates the previous one in place, keeping your library, sign-in and downloads.
  • The window takes the colours of the record or artist on the page.
  • Pages scroll under a toolbar and transport that frost as content passes beneath them.
  • Every button answers a press, and its glyph moves as its verb means: queue drops onto a line, shuffle crosses its arrows.
  • Lyrics sit in a panel you can resize, and a click on a line seeks to it.
  • On a first launch the desktop app sets up your folders, sharing them with your phone, reaching them through Tailscale, or signing in to a server.
  • The desktop app updates itself, checking each download against the digest GitHub publishes.

The rest is in the changelog.

Your server

The desktop app can serve its own library. Choose Shares its library in Settings and a QR code signs your phone in, with nothing else to install. Sharing your computer.

On a machine that stays on, run koan --headless or the container: a web UI with gapless playback, public share links, the OpenSubsonic API and MCP for assistants. It replaces Navidrome: how to move.

Inviting someone is one link or QR code, which signs a device in with a key of its own. Each part works alone: kōan's apps play from Navidrome, and Play:Sub or any other Subsonic app plays from a kōan server.

Your remote

Any kōan app controls any other, directly on the same network or from anywhere through a kōan server. The phone is the remote for the desktop, and the desktop for the Apple TV. Move here brings the queue to the device in your hand, and each device plays its own copy of the music. Devices.

UPnP amplifiers get the original file. An assistant can drive it too: the server speaks MCP with its own sign-in, and nothing reached through it can move, rename or delete a file. MCP.

Your EQ

A correction belongs to an output, so plugging in the headphones selects theirs. A device with nothing chosen runs nothing and plays untouched.

AutoEQ finds a headphone by name. Equalizer APO, CamillaDSP, REW and Roon filters import as they are, with convolution on the phone as on the desktop. Bands are edited on a response graph, an EQ can follow you to every device on a kōan server, and a UPnP amp can be sent a processed stream. EQ and convolution.

On every device

  • Mac: Koan.dmg, macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon, or brew install --cask radiosilence/tap/koan-app.
  • Linux: an AppImage and a .deb for x86-64 and ARM64 with each release.
  • iPhone, iPad and Apple TV: TestFlight, iOS or tvOS 26 or later. A TV signs in from your phone, with no password to type.
  • Android: koan-android-arm64.apk on an arm64 phone running Android 10 or later.
  • Terminal: mise use -g github:radiosilence/koan@latest, brew install radiosilence/tap/koan or cargo install koan-cli.
  • Server: the same binary or ghcr.io/radiosilence/koan. Running a server.

To look around first, sign in to demo.koan.rocks as demo, password demo, from any of the apps or a browser. Getting started covers the first run.
