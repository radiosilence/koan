kōan

It's a music player. Designed for both local and remote collections (subsonic/navidrome). Remote works with a fairly aggressive local cache. It is super fast and handles 1TB+ libraries with ease and has all the things you'd want like gapless, queue management, "bit-perfect" (so much as the audio stack allows), combined search...etc. Built from 25 years of experience messing about with music and being annoyed with pretty much everything and wanting my dream application. There are some organisational features such as file renaming, which is compatible with fb2k syntax, and I plan to add a decent well thought out tagger once I have pondered the UX more.

Originally built as a Rust TUI and core, I've now added a beautiful macOS SwiftUI app (no Electron) that uses FFI to bridge to the rust. It's fast, it's pretty, it has these lush transitions, and the point is to do all the basics properly and well before adding features, I'm really proud of it. The UX is somewhat inspired by taking the things I like about Apple Music and fb2k, but also fixing things I thought were dumb.

Full disclaimer: AI assisted coding was used. I have been building somewhat high quality software for a long time (decades) before AI existed, and I'd like to think the decisions I've been making reflect this as opposed to just vibing slop. I probably could have written it myself, but I kind of wanted to take a step back and be more of an architect/technical lead/product owner rather than a coder for this.

— @radiosilence

What is unusual about it

  • The DAC runs at the track's rate. Each track switches the device to its own sample rate; nothing is resampled to suit the device, and the format badge says so when a device refuses. Playback
  • Gapless survives editing the queue. The decoder queues the next track early, and if you then reorder or remove it, playback restarts at the playhead with your edit rather than playing what was queued. Playback
  • Room and headphone correction per output. Roon filter packs, REW and rePhase impulses, CamillaDSP and Equalizer APO configs and AutoEQ import as they are, and a device without a profile runs nothing at all. Equalisation and convolution
  • Network amplifiers get the original file. A UPnP/DLNA renderer is handed the file itself, or, with a correction profile, one FLAC stream for the whole queue, gapless whether or not the amplifier can do gapless. Amplifiers and streamers
  • Music moves between devices seamlessly. It picks up exactly where it stopped, at once if the other device has the track, with none of AirPlay's buffering. That device plays its own copy, so it keeps going if your phone sleeps or loses signal. Devices on one network control each other, whoever is signed in, and a device can be shared with another account on your server to control from anywhere. One device at a time: no multiroom yet. Controlling and moving
  • Built for losing signal on the Tube. While there is signal, the queue is fetched ahead of the playhead, the current track first, as far as the cache limit allows, so it keeps playing underground; anything played once plays again offline. A long queue cannot push the cache past its limit. Cache management
  • Fast sync. A full sync runs at about a second per 10,000 tracks on an iPhone against a kōan server: songs are listed a page at a time, about a hundred requests for fifty thousand tracks rather than one per album. A server whose library has not changed is not walked at all. Remote servers
  • A whole ecosystem that is still plain OpenSubsonic. Apps, terminal UI, web UI, share links and server all come from kōan, but the apps play from Navidrome too, and Play:Sub or any other Subsonic app plays from a kōan server. As a server
  • Light on the machine. Nothing polls: the player sleeps until something happens, and the analyser stops when nothing draws it. Playing, with a 51,000-track library loaded, the Mac app uses about 200 MB and 1% of one core; the server holding that library idles at 30 MB and a thousandth of a core. The app is 33 MB installed; the command line is a single 45 MB binary. The pieces
  • An assistant as a remote. Add the server to Claude as a connector and sign in with your kōan account; there is no gateway or identity provider. It plays on the phone or the Mac you name, and builds playlists that reach every device and hold places for albums still downloading, filled when they land. It can never move, rename or delete a file, whatever the account. With an assistant
  • Inviting someone is one link. Opened on a phone or Mac with kōan, it signs that device in with a key of its own and syncs the library, with nothing to type. Each device's key can be revoked on its own, and changing the password withdraws every link already sent. Invites
  • One Rust core, linked in. The SwiftUI apps on macOS and iOS call the audio engine in-process: no Electron, and no daemon or port between the interface and the sound. The pieces

How kōan compares sets these against NaviBeat, Navidrome, Jellyfin, Roon and the rest, feature by feature, including what kōan does not do.

The basics, done properly

  • Compilations stay one album. Albums are grouped by album artist, so a various-artists record does not split into one album per track artist, and two editions with different MusicBrainz releases stay two.
  • The queue comes back as you left it. After a restart it is all there, at the point in the track where you stopped, and it plays only if it was playing.
  • A track can be queued twice. Every entry has its own id, so removing or moving one copy leaves the other alone, and the last 100 queue edits can be undone.
  • One artist is one artist. SIGUR RÓS on a server and Sigur Rós on disk are the same act, whatever the case or Unicode form.
  • Your tags are left alone. kōan reads them and never writes them back; favourites belong to the track, so they follow it when the file moves.

The pieces

Every part of kōan is the same Rust core with a different front on it. The apps link the core in-process; nothing between the interface and the audio engine is a network hop.

  • macOS app. SwiftUI, for the machine you listen at. Plays local folders, a server, or both merged into one library.
  • iOS app. The same SwiftUI app in a phone's shell. A phone has no music folder, so it plays from a server, and keeps what it has played in a local cache.
  • Terminal UI and CLI. koan on macOS and Linux: the player in a terminal, plus scanning, search, file organisation and the server itself.
  • Server. koan --headless, or the container image, on the machine that holds the music. It serves a web UI, share links, an OpenSubsonic API and MCP.

For a single computer with the music on it, the app on its own is enough. To listen from a phone, another computer or a browser, run the server where the music lives and point everything else at it. The apps sync its library, stream what they play and cache it, so a record played once plays again with no signal. Any Subsonic client works against it as well.

Install

macOS app

Download Koan.dmg, open it and drag kōan into Applications. It is signed and notarised. Or with Homebrew:

brew install --cask radiosilence/koan/koan-app

You may have to update Homebrew's trust settings to trust the tap.

Requires macOS 26 or later.

iOS app

Build it from source with Xcode and install it on a phone plugged into the Mac, signed with your own (free) Apple developer team:

git clone https://github.com/radiosilence/koan.git && cd koan
just ios-phone

Requires iOS 26 or later, and a kōan, Navidrome or other Subsonic server to play from.

Command line and terminal UI

# mise (recommended)
mise use -g github:radiosilence/koan@latest

# homebrew
brew install radiosilence/koan/koan

# or via cargo
cargo install koan-cli

# or build from source
git clone https://github.com/radiosilence/koan.git && cd koan
cargo install --path crates/koan-cli

A single binary. macOS needs nothing else. Linux needs the ALSA and D-Bus development headers:

# Debian/Ubuntu
sudo apt install libasound2-dev libdbus-1-dev

# Fedora
sudo dnf install alsa-lib-devel dbus-devel

# Arch
sudo pacman -S alsa-lib dbus

Setting it up

One computer

In the macOS app, everything is in Settings: Library points kōan at your music and scans it, and playback and devices have their own panes. No terminal is needed.

From the command line:

koan config init   # create the config dir and a commented template
# edit ~/.config/koan/config.local.toml:
#   [library]
#   folders = ["/path/to/your/music"]
koan scan          # index your library
koan               # launch the TUI

space pauses, < and > skip, p picks tracks, a picks albums, q quits.

A server for everything else

Run the container on the machine with the music. It needs no sound card, keeps its config, index and keys in /config, and only ever reads the library.

docker run -d --name koan -p 4000:4000 \
  -v koan-config:/config \
  -v /path/to/music:/music:ro \
  -e 'KOAN_LIBRARY__FOLDERS=["/music"]' \
  -e 'KOAN_GRAPHQL__ALLOWED_HOSTS=["music.example.com"]' \
  -e KOAN_SHARING__PUBLIC_URL=https://music.example.com \
  -e KOAN_SUBSONIC__ENABLED=true \
  ghcr.io/radiosilence/koan:latest

docker exec -it koan koan auth setup   # the admin account

Put it behind a reverse proxy with TLS at the hostname you listed, or use the Docker Compose file, which runs Caddy in front and gets the certificate itself. The server refuses any Host it was not given, and share links are built on public_url. The first admin can only be made from a shell on the server, as above; after that, add people from the web UI's Users page or with koan auth create-user. Without a container, koan --headless --bind 0.0.0.0 does the same with the config in ~/.config/koan; Running a server has the details, and Migrating from Navidrome translates a Navidrome setup.

Then sign in from each client with a kōan account:

  • Browser: open the hostname.
  • macOS and iOS apps: Settings → Server.
  • Terminal: koan remote login https://music.example.com <user>, then koan remote sync.
  • Other Subsonic apps: the same URL and account.

A Navidrome or other Subsonic server works in the kōan server's place, for the apps and terminal UI; the web UI, share pages, linked apps and MCP are kōan's own. Local and remote tracks merge into one library, local files take priority for playback, and remote tracks stream while they download.

On Kubernetes

Each release publishes a Pulumi component at the same version, @radiosilence/koan-pulumi, on GitHub Packages. It builds the Deployment, its Service and a NetworkPolicy that admits only your ingress:

# .npmrc (GitHub Packages wants a token even for public packages)
@radiosilence:registry=https://npm.pkg.github.com
import { createKoan } from "@radiosilence/koan-pulumi";

createKoan(provider, "media", {
  hostname: "music.example.com",
  library: { hostPath: "/mnt/music" },
  state: { hostPath: "/var/lib/koan" },
  nodeSelector: { "kubernetes.io/hostname": "media-node" },
});

Route the hostname to the koan Service with whatever ingress you run. Pin the package version and the server follows it: the image tag is the package's version.

Filing new music

kōan reads tags as they are and does not rewrite them. sift, a separate tool, matches a download against MusicBrainz, tags it, embeds the cover and files it into the library, reading an existing beets config. A server watching that folder picks the album up on its own.

mise use -g github:radiosilence/sift
sift import ~/Downloads/some-album

What it does

Playback

  • Bit-perfect output through CoreAudio on macOS and ALSA on Linux. The device is switched to the source's sample rate rather than resampled to reach it; when a device refuses, the format badge says the output is resampled.
  • Gapless: the decoder runs ahead across track boundaries.
  • Shuffle and repeat, of the whole queue or one track. Repeats are gapless, and each pass counts in history. Shuffle reorders the queue you see, and turning it off puts the queue back in its original order.
  • Network amplifiers and streamers: the Mac app and the terminal UI play to UPnP/DLNA renderers, such as a WiiM or an Arcam amplifier. The queue stays in kōan and the renderer is sent the original file, so playback is bit-perfect up to its DAC, and gapless where the renderer supports it. Give an amplifier its own EQ or convolution profile and kōan processes the queue itself and sends it one continuous FLAC stream instead, gapless whatever the amplifier supports. See Playing on another device.
  • EQ and convolution, chosen per output device, so headphones get their correction only when they are the output. Profiles import from what other tools write: Roon and Convolver configs, impulse responses from REW or rePhase, CamillaDSP and Equalizer APO (mixers, delays and graphic EQ included), and AutoEQ. A device without a profile runs nothing and stays bit-perfect, and the format badge says when audio is processed. See Equalisation and convolution.
  • Sleep timer: after 15 to 60 minutes, fading out, or at the end of the track or record. It pauses and keeps the queue, and a phone controlling a Mac sets the Mac's. See Sleep timer.
  • Formats: FLAC, MP3, AAC, Vorbis, Opus, ALAC, ADPCM, WAV, AIFF and CAF, in Ogg, Matroska/WebM and MP4 containers.
  • ReplayGain in track and album modes, with peak limiting and a configurable pre-amp.
  • Media keys and now-playing through Control Center on macOS and MPRIS on Linux.

Library

  • Local and Subsonic/Navidrome in one library, synced when the server changes, with streaming and favourites and playlists synced both ways.
  • A download cache with a size limit that holds however long the queue is: tracks are fetched ahead of the cursor as far as the limit allows, played ones are evicted first, and tracks downloaded to keep go last. See Cache management.
  • Search across the whole library with SQLite full-text search.
  • Queue grouped by album, with drag reordering, multi-select, 100 levels of undo, and Finder drag and drop. It survives restarts.
  • Playlists, ordered and named, exportable as M3U8. Smart playlists hold whatever matches a set of rules, and Navidrome's .nsp files and .m3u files in the library folders are imported. See Smart playlists.
  • Play history and Recently played, shared between the devices of one account on a kōan server. Forgetting a play forgets it on all of them.
  • Synced lyrics from LRCLIB, with the current line highlighted.
  • File organisation using sift's foobar2000-compatible format strings (%field%, [conditionals] and 59 $functions()). Every move is previewed, collisions included, before anything is touched.

Automation

  • GraphQL API for full control alongside the app or TUI, or headless as a music server.
  • Subsonic-compatible API, so other Subsonic clients can play from a kōan server.
  • MCP for assistants: at /mcp on a server, signing in with a kōan account, or koan mcp for a desktop client. See below.
  • Accounts with Ed25519 JWTs, three roles and 1Password CLI integration.

Not there yet: tag editing.

The macOS app

A SwiftUI app built for macOS 26. Browse and search the library (⌘K), build and reorder the queue, keep playlists, favourite tracks, albums and artists, read synced lyrics, look through play history and what was played recently, set a sleep timer and reorganise files on disk. The Output menu sends the music to this Mac's audio devices or to UPnP amplifiers on the network, each with its own EQ or convolution preset, and marks the speaker button while what is heard is processed. Control, once there is another kōan device, drives that device instead, and Output then lists its outputs. First-run setup covers library folders and remote sign-in.

It links the Rust core in-process through FFI rather than talking to a server, so there is no daemon, port or auth surface between the interface and the audio engine, and CoreAudio output never leaves Rust. It shares one library and one config with the terminal UI: a queue saved in one shows up in the other.

Two things it leaves to the terminal: visualisers, and running the server (koan --headless).

The iOS app

The macOS app's pages and engine in a phone's shell: a tab bar for the queue, the library, search and settings, and Now Playing with synced lyrics and AirPlay over the playing record's colour. It plays from a server and caches what it plays, so the rest of an album or queue carries on underground. On an iPad with room for a sidebar it takes the Mac's layout. Output goes through the system mixer, so bit-perfect is a claim for the Mac and Linux only. EQ and convolution profiles follow the route, so AirPods and wired headphones each get their own.

The terminal UI

A full-screen Ratatui interface that runs on macOS and Linux: a transport bar with album art, an album-grouped queue, a fuzzy picker, a library browser, a lyrics panel, mouse support and 22 visualiser modes.

As a server

Run kōan on the machine that holds the music (koan --headless, or a container) and it serves the library to everything else. It stands in for Navidrome rather than needing it; Migrating from Navidrome covers the move.

  • Web UI. Every album, artist and track on one page, search and a queue, laid out for a phone as well as a desktop. Filters apply as you type and live in the URL, so a filtered view can be bookmarked or sent. Favourites, Recently played and History are there as in the apps, with hearts and a track menu on every row. Playback is gapless, in the browser.
  • Share links. Sharing a track shares its album, cued to that track. An album shares the album, and an artist their albums. The page plays without an account, and a pasted link unfurls with its cover.
  • OpenSubsonic API. Subsonic apps such as Play:Sub, and kōan's own app and terminal UI on other machines, connect with a kōan account: by password, with an API key, or with an app password for apps that only sign in with a token. Ratings and bookmarks are kept per account, and a stream is transcoded to Opus, MP3 or AAC for an app that asks for a lower bitrate.
  • Scrobbling. Each account can connect ListenBrainz, and the server sends every play its apps report, so no app needs setting up. See Scrobbling.
  • Accounts. Admin, user and read-only roles, and API keys and app passwords that can be revoked from the web UI's Account page.
  • Linked apps. kōan's Mac and iPhone apps keep a connection open to a kōan server, which makes each one a remote the server can drive: see what it is playing and its queue, start an album on it, queue, pause and skip. Playlist edits and library changes reach every device without a sync, and one that is away receives them when it next connects. A plain Subsonic server can only answer what an app asks.
  • MCP. At /mcp, with the server as its own sign-in, so an assistant can browse the library and drive playback on any of the person's linked devices. See With an assistant.

With an assistant

A kōan server is an MCP server. Claude, or any assistant that speaks MCP, works with your library and with the kōan apps you listen on, as you. It sees every phone and computer linked to your account, what each is playing and what is queued, and sends commands to whichever you name.

What it is good for

  • Playing on your devices. "Play something like Polar Bear on my phone." It finds the music in your library and starts it on the iPhone; "pause the desktop", "skip this", "play the Phace remix next" go to the device you mean. A phone that iOS has put to sleep gets a notification to tap, and queue changes wait for it to wake.
  • Making playlists that reach every device. "Make me a playlist for a long drive, mostly instrumental, nothing over seven minutes." It researches what fits, picks it from your library in order, and the playlist appears on your Mac and phone straight away, no sync needed.
  • Playlists that fill as the music arrives. For picks your library does not have yet, it can leave a place in the playlist: when the album reaches your library, the tracks it wanted are added. You hear what is there now, and the rest turns up in the playlist when it lands.
  • Knowing what is on. "What's this song?" and "what's left in the queue on my phone?" read the device's own state.
  • Sharing. "Make a link to this album I can send a friend" creates a share page that plays without an account, once you have said yes to it.

Connecting

In Claude, open Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector and enter your server's address with /mcp on the end:

https://music.example.com/mcp

Claude opens a page on your server. Sign in with your kōan account, check that it names claude.ai, and allow it. Other assistants that support OAuth connect the same way, and the server's web UI has an Assistants page with the address to copy. No gateway or identity provider is involved: the server is its own sign-in, and needs public_url set to the address it is reached at.

What it can and cannot do

A connection acts as the account that approved it, on that account's devices and playlists. It can never move, rename or delete a file, or change where the library is. Those are refused through MCP whatever the account, because track titles and tags reach the model, and any of them could carry an instruction. An admin account connects with ordinary rights for the same reason. Its tokens work at /mcp and nowhere else.

Without a server, koan mcp runs the player on this machine for a desktop client such as Claude Desktop. The MCP guide covers both.
